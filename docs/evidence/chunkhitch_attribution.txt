READING:
- The mesh never exceeds ~13 ms. CONFIRMED not the hitch (matches T0).
- A single buffer_create stalls up to 25.6 ms; a single buffer_realloc up to 32.2 ms. avg is
  sub-millisecond => these are RARE TAIL OUTLIERS, concentrated in the initial ~222-chunk DB
  world-load allocation burst (the fresh 64-chunk gen did 120 more reallocs and beat NONE of these
  maxes, and avg fell — i.e. the big stalls are early / first-touch, not per-realloc-recurring).
- The steady per-frame chunk-update (dirty_update_total) is <=11.8 ms while actively generating —
  UNDER budget. Streaming/generation chunk-update is NOT the hitch.

================================================================================
ATTRIBUTION VERDICT
================================================================================
The >=30 ms chunk-update HITCH (as distinct from render density and the bulk-gen job) is a single
GPU BUFFER ALLOCATION/REALLOCATION stall: buffer_create up to 25.6 ms, buffer_realloc up to 32.2 ms,
directly measured. It is a RARE TAIL, concentrated in allocation bursts (DB world-load; would recur
on a heavy streaming influx and on buffer growth), and appears partly first-touch/driver-warmup.
The mesh (<=13 ms) and steady per-frame chunk-update (<=12 ms) are NOT the hitch.

Scene-dependence (reconciles T0):
- LIGHT scene (T0 flat StructGenTest, render <16 ms): a buffer alloc/realloc stall IS a visible
  ~40 ms edit hitch (T0's first-edit + growth-edit stutters).
- DENSE scene (this session, render ~32 ms): the same stall is masked; the dominant frame cost is
  render density (#1 issue), a SEPARATE track from this plan.

IMPLICATIONS FOR B1/B2 (re-prioritised, honestly):
1. The buffer-alloc hitch is real but MODEST and RARE relative to what was assumed; its user-visible
   impact is largest on light scenes / edit-heavy sessions and during load/streaming bursts.
2. B2's motive is stronger than B1's frame-timing motive: ~1-1.6k resident chunks x up to 3 raw
   vkAllocateMemory approaches maxMemoryAllocationCount (~4096) = a latent CRASH/scaling bug, and the
   inline free of an in-flight buffer in reallocateBuffer is a real use-after-free (correctness).
   A suballocator fixes the hitch tail, the alloc-count ceiling, AND the UAF in one structural change.
3. On dense scenes the bigger fish is render density (#1), which this plan does NOT address.

================================================================================
B1 FOLLOW-UP FINDING — the buffer stall is ONE-TIME COLD FIRST-TOUCH, not recurring
================================================================================
After adding the B1 deferred-free (default ON), a fresh Release run:
  - initial 222-chunk DB load: buffer_realloc MAX 47.95 ms, buffer_create MAX 1.13 ms (one cold spike)
  - then generated 64 MORE chunks on the now-WARMED device (72 new creates, 125 new reallocs):
      buffer_create  max UNCHANGED 1.13 ms   (all 72 new creates fast)
      buffer_realloc max UNCHANGED 47.95 ms  (all 125 new reallocs faster than the load spike)
      buffer_realloc avg 0.49 ms
  => The 25-48 ms buffer alloc/realloc stalls are ONE-TIME COLD FIRST-TOUCH device-allocation costs
     concentrated at the initial world-load (driver/OS committing the first VkDeviceMemory pages).
     They DO NOT recur once the heap is warm; post-warmup creates/reallocs are sub-2 ms.

Reconciles T0: the ~40 ms "first edit after load" stutter was this same cold first-touch (first
realloc/create warming the path); later edits were smooth.

CONSEQUENCE for B1:
  - Deferred-free (B1) fixes a genuine USE-AFTER-FREE (reallocateBuffer freeing a buffer still bound
    in an in-flight frame; MAX_FRAMES_IN_FLIGHT=2) — a real correctness bug, worth fixing.
  - But it does NOT remove the cold-first-touch stall (that is alloc-side, one-time, load-only; a
    deferred FREE cannot help a fresh vkAllocateMemory warmup). B1 is a CORRECTNESS fix, not a perf
    fix. There is essentially no recurring buffer stall to remove.
  - The higher-value latent issue remains B2's alloc-count ceiling (~1-1.6k chunks x up to 3 raw
    vkAllocateMemory -> ~4096), a crash/scaling risk independent of frame timing.
