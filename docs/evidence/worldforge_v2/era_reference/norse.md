## 6. What is unsourced, and cost of a full pack

**Unsourced after this pass (must be found before a pack starts):**

1. Every opening: door width/height, window size, smoke-hole size.
2. Every height except Lofotr (9 m, rebuild) and Borgund (11 m): eaves height, wall height, ridge height, roof pitch.
3. Structural module: roof-post spacing (bay), aisle widths, bench width and height.
4. Town layout: plot width and depth at Hedeby, Ribe, Kaupang, Dublin; York and Norwegian figures are snippet-only; street/lane widths everywhere.
5. Pit house: no Norse dimension at all.
6. Naust: the one fully measured example read is 12th–13th century.

**Where the numbers probably are (titles from memory — NOT opened and NOT verified in this pass; check each
citation before relying on it):** Schietzel, *Stand der siedlungsarchäologischen Forschung in
Haithabu* (1981) and *Spurensuche Haithabu* (2014); Wallace, *The Viking Age Buildings of Dublin* (1992); Hall et al.,
*Anglo-Scandinavian Occupation at 16–22 Coppergate: Defining a Townscape* (York Archaeological Trust AY 8/5, 2014,
open access); Skre (ed.), *Kaupang in Skiringssal* (2007, open access); Olsen & Schmidt, *Fyrkat I* (1977); Nørlund,
*Trelleborg* (1948); Roussell, in *Forntida gårdar i Island* (1943) for Stöng; Komber, Schmidt and others on house
reconstruction; Grimm, *Großbootshaus* (2006) for naust; Riksantikvaren measured drawings for the stave churches.

Overall: a farmstead-and-small-town Norse pack is **moderate** — less new geometry than Edo or pueblo, but the
dimension debt is the largest of the three because the excavation monographs are not online as text.
